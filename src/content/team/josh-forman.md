Josh is a business executive with more than 20 years spent building and running
defense and intelligence businesses end to end: developing scalable solutions,
winning the work, standing up the team, and owning the delivery that follows.
His career has consistently landed where solutions development meets the
mission that funds it. He built a Department of War business unit from scratch
for an artificial intelligence and machine learning software firm, directed
special projects under a $1B agile software development task order spanning
more than 56 intelligence applications, and delivered capability for the
Department of War that had never been built before. That customer fluency is
first-hand rather than briefed, which is why he is equally at ease in a board
meeting, on a J2 staff, and in an engineering review.

Josh began his career in Navy intelligence, delivering time-critical
indications and warnings during Operation Enduring Freedom and Operation Iraqi
Freedom. That work included direct support to the 2006 non-combatant evacuation
of American citizens from Lebanon and humanitarian assistance operations in
Nicaragua in 2007. He went on to support special operations units conducting
time-sensitive operations around the globe.

Service to the community that shaped his career is his primary driver. Josh
serves on the board of directors of the Donovan & Bank Foundation, a 501(c)(3)
in Fayetteville, North Carolina, that helps active-duty Special Forces
operators and their spouses close the warrior story and step into a life of
balance. His governance responsibility spans all three of the foundation's
lines of effort: the JANUS workshop program, the Special Operations fundraising
events, and wellness programming that builds individualized care paths for
operators carrying the cost of long service. He also mentors beneficiaries of
the Special Operations Warrior Foundation, the CIA Memorial Foundation, No
Greater Sacrifice, and the Defense Intelligence Memorial Foundation, working
alongside the children and surviving families of those lost in service as they
navigate education, early career, and the move into the post-college workforce.
